## What this repository is

The dissertation reports four studies, presented as Chapters IV to VII. The supplementary tables, figures, methods and data for those four studies are held here rather than bound into the dissertation, which cites them by file number in the form `Supplementary File 3, Table S4`.

Tables and figures inside a supplementary file are numbered independently of those in the dissertation, so `Table S4` always means the fourth supplementary table of that file and never a table in the main text.

### Supplementary File 1 — Chapter IV

*Resistance-associated substitution profiles outperform whole-genome sequences for machine learning prediction of hepatitis C treatment failure across multiple genotypes*

GenBank accessions and provenance for all 162 sequences analysed, and the sixteen patients represented by two sequences; hyperparameter search spaces and selected values for the 13 classifiers; direct-acting antiviral regimen and outcome by genotype and by subtype; NS5A and NS3 polymorphism profiles across the genotype 6 subtypes; the substitutions significantly associated with failure; and complete performance metrics for all 65 model and feature-set configurations. Includes the TRIPOD+AI reporting checklist.

### Supplementary File 2 — Chapter V

*Refining PCR primers for hepatitis C virus subtypes with limited public sequences: a strategy applied to subtype 6f*

Per-position sequence conservation and the availability of candidate primer sites; the 140 public genotype 6 sequences used in the cross-subtype analysis; reaction composition; the eleven public subtype 6f sequences available at design time; the methods in full; and per-position conservation of both amplicons as a data file.

### Supplementary File 3 — Chapter VI

*Sofosbuvir/velpatasvir failure in hepatitis C subtype 6f: a paired virological analysis before and after therapy*

Primers and thermal cycling; coverage of the reference-coordinate consensus sequences; comparison of sequenced against non-sequenced subtype 6f failures; read depth and limit of detection at every scored codon, with robustness to the minor-variant threshold; change in resistance-residue frequency at each NS5A drug-target position; the lineage residue at every codon of NS5A and NS5B; diversity and selection by gene and time point; the per-patient clinical and virological master table; every resistance call generated by consensus interpretation with its read-level adjudication; two-amplicon concordance; and the clinical characteristics of all 124 genotyped failures by subtype.

### Supplementary File 4 — Chapter VII

*Patient risk and viral genetics: two levels of risk for sofosbuvir–velpatasvir failure in hepatitis C elimination*

Baseline characteristics of the clinical layer; multiple imputation of the multivariable model; the joint effect of cirrhosis and ribavirin, and ribavirin after propensity score adjustment; E-values and partial-identification bounds for the host and the subtype associations; the NS5A-28 scoring definition fork; pre-specified sensitivity analyses and the district-adjusted primary test; population attributable fractions; paired pre-treatment and post-failure samples at NS5A-28; PERMANOVA and principal coordinates analyses with and without codon 28; NS5B-282 read-level adjudication; and the sublineage check. The analysis workbook accompanies this file.

## How to cite

Cite the archive by its DOI:

> Le DHH. Supplementary material for *Hepatitis C virus resistance evolution under direct-acting antiviral pressure: a clinical and genomic profile of treatment failure in a Thai cohort* [Data set]. Zenodo. doi:[DOI to be inserted]

To cite one file, add the file number, for example `Supplementary File 3`.

## Nucleotide sequences

Sequences generated by these studies are deposited in GenBank; accession numbers are listed in the relevant supplementary file. Sequence records are not duplicated here.

## Data and privacy

The clinical tables are reported at the level of de-identified specimen codes. They carry no name, hospital number, national identification number, address or date of birth, and no record in this archive can be traced to an individual without the key held by the originating sites.

The studies were approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Faculty of Medicine, Chulalongkorn University (certificates 0038/69 and 0111/68), under parent protocols 0918/67 and 028/63.
